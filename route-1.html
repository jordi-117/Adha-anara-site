Birds of the Rural Mosaic in La Chaquira

Description

You will explore a region where ancient history coexists with birds native to the upper Magdalena River. Along the way, you will observe Andean species while enjoying the river landscapes and the agricultural areas that characterize the region. This experience highlights the importance of conservation and how it fosters the presence of birds in the area. Their colors, songs, and movements will accompany you throughout the journey, becoming the central element of the adventure.

Includes

  • Binoculars
  • Breakfast
  • Snacks and hydration
  • Birdwatching guide
  • Medical insurance
  • Photos and checklist
  • Private transportation (optional)

Details

  • Duration: Approximately 4 hours
  • Hiking distance: 4 km (round trip)
  • Start time: 6:00 am
  • Difficulty: Moderate
  • Altitude: 1,722 m.a.s.l.
  • Average number of species: 30–50

Location

Hamlet La Chaquira, Municipality of San Agustín, Department of Huila, Colombia

Best time

Year round

Group size

Max 6 people

What to bring

Comfortable hiking shoes, warm jacket, hat, sunscreen, mosquito repellent

Climate

Cool to cold in altitude, occasional rain, warm days most of the time

Ecosystems

Secondary forests, Cloud forests, agricultural landscapes

Professional guide

Professional birding guide with extensive local knowledge

Types of Plant Cover

The cover is a Rural Mosaic, composed of remnants of sub-Andean forest (cloud forest), shade-grown coffee plantations and grazing areas.

Tour Plan

01
5:50 a.m.

Departure for the way of La Chaquira. We meet at the agreed pickup point and head out through the quiet rural roads as the first light of dawn begins to appear over the mountains.

02
6:00 a.m.

Arrival at the beginning of the tour in the way of La Chaquira. The guide will introduce the area, explain the route ahead, and provide a brief overview of the bird species most likely to be encountered at this unique rural mosaic ecosystem.

03
6:10 a.m.

Beginning of the birdwatching activity. This is the most active time of day for birds. We move slowly through the trail, scanning the treetops and forest edges for endemic species such as the Indigo-capped Hummingbird and the Red-billed Emerald. Every sound and movement is an opportunity.

04
9:00 a.m.

Breakfast break. We pause to enjoy a traditional breakfast in the field, surrounded by the sounds of the countryside. A perfect moment to review the species recorded so far on the checklist, share sightings with the group, and recharge energy for the rest of the morning.

05
10:00 a.m.

End of the birdwatching activity. We wrap up the morning session with a final review of the species checklist. The guide will share interesting facts about the birds observed and answer any questions before the group prepares to head back.

06
10:00 a.m.

Return to the hotel. After a fulfilling day of birdwatching, we make our way back to the hotel. Time to rest, upload your photos, and relive the highlights of a truly memorable experience in the Colombian Andes.

Key Bird Species

This route offers excellent opportunities to observe colorful tanagers, iridescent hummingbirds, shy antpittas, and incredible toucans. You may encounter endemic species found only in Colombian highlands, including specialized moorland birds and cloud forest specialists. Photography opportunities are excellent throughout the day.
