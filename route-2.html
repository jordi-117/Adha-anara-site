Nature Reserve El Oso - On Summits and Fog

Description

Discover the diversity of the Andean forests in a private reserve with decades of protection. Along the trail, you can observe striking birds such as the Andean Tanager, the Crested Quetzal, and various species of Chlorophonia, as well as enjoy orchids lining the paths. Depending on the season, it is also possible to see the Andean Cock-of-the-rock. With a bit of luck, you might spot signs of or sightings of mammals native to the area, such as the Woolly Monkey, the Ocelot, or the Spectacled Bear.

Includes

  • Binoculars
  • Breakfast, lunch, and dinner
  • Snacks and hydration
  • Birdwatching guide
  • Medical insurance
  • Photos and checklist
  • Private transportation (round trip)
  • Accommodation (1 night)

Details

  • Duration: Approximately 4 hours
  • Hiking distance: 2.2 km
  • Start time: 6:00 am
  • Difficulty: Moderate
  • Altitude: 2,380 m.a.s.l.
  • Average number of species: 30–50

Location

Nature Reserve Portal del Oso, Hamlet El Oso, Municipality of San Agustín, Department of Huila, Colombia

Best time

Year round

Group size

Max 6 people

What to bring

Comfortable hiking shoes, warm jacket, hat, sunscreen, mosquito repellent

Climate

Cool to cold in altitude, occasional rain, warm days most of the time

Ecosystems

Andean cloud forest, primary and secondary

Professional guide

Professional birding guide with extensive local knowledge

Types of Plant Cover

Andean cloud forest. Reserve forest cover, homogeneous and with minimal intervention compared to the rural mosaic.

Tour Plan

01
4:00 p.m.

Departure toward Nature Reserve Portal del Oso. We meet at the agreed pickup point and head into the mountains as the last lights of dusk filters through the cloud forest.

02
5:30 p.m.

Arrival at the reserve entrance. The guide introduces the area, the rules of the reserve, and gives a preview of the key species that inhabit this Andean cloud forest, including the Crested Quetzal and Wattled Guan.

03
6:00 a.m.

Start of the birdwatching activity. Moving slowly through the trail, we scan orchid-lined paths and dense canopy for Flame-faced Tanagers, Chlorophonia species, and with luck, the Andean Cock-of-the-rock. Every bend in the trail offers a new surprise.

04
9:00 a.m.

Breakfast break inside the reserve. A moment to rest, share sightings, and review the checklist while enjoying a traditional breakfast surrounded by the sounds and atmosphere of the Andean forest.

05
10:00 a.m. – 12:00 p.m.

Continuation of birdwatching along upper trail sections, exploring higher elevations of the reserve where different species appear. Look out for the Crimson-crested Woodpecker and Cinnamon Flycatcher in this zone.

06
12:30 p.m.

Lunch at the reserve. Time to rest and enjoy a hearty lunch while the midday heat settles. A great opportunity to process photos, note observations, and prepare for the afternoon session.

07
2:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m.

Afternoon birdwatching session. The late afternoon brings new activity as birds feed before dusk. This is an ideal time to spot mammals such as the Woolly Monkey or Ocelot that may cross the trail.

08
4:30 p.m.

Return to San Agustín. End of the full-day andean mountain adventure with a memorable checklist of Rare and Endangered specialties.

Key Bird Species

This route offers extraordinary opportunities to observe cloud forest specialists, including the Crested Quetzal, Wattled Guan, and Flame-faced Tanager, within a decades-old private reserve. Orchids line the paths while mammals such as the Woolly Monkey, Ocelot, and Spectacled Bear may also be spotted. Photography opportunities are outstanding throughout the day.
